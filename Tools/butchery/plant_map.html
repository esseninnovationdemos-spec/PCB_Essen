Kill Floor to Cold Store

A thirteen-chamber pork plant laid out around the one rule that governs every slaughterhouse: product moves one way and never doubles back, and the dirty side never touches the clean side.

02

Why it is shaped like this

Three constraints do almost all the work. Everything else is consequence.

Product never doubles back

The flow is a flattened S: east along the kill floor, down into chilling, then west through cutting, packing and cold store to the dock. Nothing chilled passes back through anything warm, which is why dispatch is at the far end rather than next to the yard where the lorries already are.

The clean/dirty line falls at evisceration

Everything up to and including singeing is dirty — live animals, hair, scald water. The carcass becomes clean the moment it is opened, which is exactly why inspection sits at evisceration and why the byproduct room hangs directly off it rather than off the cutting hall. Offal drops through a chute one floor level, and never crosses a clean corridor to get there.

Rendering touches nothing edible

Inedible material goes from byproduct to rendering by enclosed screw. Rendering has its own external door and shares no opening with a clean chamber. It sits on the dirty side of the building even though the material reaching it came from the clean side, because once condemned it is dirty again.

What the plan is not

A layout for building. It is dimensionally coherent and follows real hygiene zoning, but it has no drainage falls, no door schedule, no air-pressure cascade between chambers, and no escape routes. It is a plan to populate a level from, and the numbers are consistent enough that the level will not need re-laying when the last models arrive.
